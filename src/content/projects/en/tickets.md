## Problem

A small support team often splits its incidents between email and spreadsheets. Nobody knows for sure which ticket is open, who owns it or how long it has been waiting, and priorities are decided from memory.

## Solution

A dashboard where every incident is a ticket with a clear lifecycle: open, assigned, in progress and resolved. The project revolves around three features:

- **Ticket lifecycle:** creation, assignment to an agent, status changes and comments, with a history of every change.
- **Metrics panel:** open tickets by priority, workload per agent and average resolution time.
- **Filters and search:** by status, priority, agent and text, to reach a ticket without scrolling through the whole list.

There will be two roles: requester, who opens tickets and follows their own, and agent, who handles them.

## Technical decisions

These are the starting decisions; I will change them if development shows they don't fit.

- **REST API with Django REST Framework** and a React interface that only consumes that API, so both sides can be tested separately.
- **Statuses as a simple state machine:** the backend rejects invalid transitions (for example, reopening a resolved ticket without going through "open") instead of trusting the frontend.
- **Metrics computed on the server** with aggregate queries, so the browser doesn't download every ticket.
- **Demo data generated by a script**, fictional and reproducible.

## Limitations and lessons learned

Scope I am leaving out on purpose: no inbound email, no real-time notifications and no file attachments.

I will write the lessons learned once the project is built.
